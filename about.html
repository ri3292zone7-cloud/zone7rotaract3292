The Basics

What Rotaract actually is.

Rotaract is Rotary International's club for young adults aged 18–30. Each Rotaract club is sponsored by a local Rotary club — a partnership that brings mentorship, credibility and institutional memory — but the club itself is entirely planned, funded and run by its own members. Nobody hands a Rotaract club its agenda; the members decide what problems to tackle and how.

That distinction matters. It's why a 21-year-old college student in Baneshwor can be running a district-recognized blood donation drive, and why a Rotaractor in their first year of a job can be managing a club's annual budget. Rotaract isn't a volunteering program you sign up for — it's a leadership training ground that happens to run entirely on real community service.

Since 2020, Rotaract clubs can technically operate independently of a Rotary sponsor, but most Zone 7 clubs still keep that relationship — it's one of the most underused resources a young club has.

🤝

Run by members, for members

Every officer role — President, Secretary, Treasurer — is held by a Rotaractor, elected by the club, for a one-year term.

🎓

Service and skill-building

Projects double as leadership practice — budgeting, public speaking, negotiation and event execution, all learned on the job.

🌏

Locally rooted, globally connected

Every club sits inside a Rotary district and a worldwide network — twin clubs, international exchanges, and a shared set of values.

What Zone 7 Does

Real projects, run by real clubs, every week of the year.

Across Zone 7's nine clubs, service work spans a wide range of causes — each one logged, reported and counted toward District 3292's official club-excellence barometer. This is what a Rotary year in Zone 7 actually looks like on the ground.

🩸

Health & Blood Donation

Blood drives run with the Nepal Red Cross Society, health camps, and hygiene-awareness sessions for schoolchildren.

📚

Education & Literacy

Literacy drives, school workshops, and mentorship programs under Rotaract's Basic Education & Literacy focus area.

🌳

Environment

Tree plantations run alongside sponsoring Rotary clubs, from Tarkeshwor Nagarpalika to river and neighborhood clean-ups.

🎓

Professional Development

Career talks, entrepreneurship bootcamps and skill workshops — Rotaract's fourth Avenue of Service, and one of the most valued.

🌍

International Service

Twin-club partnerships and joint virtual events — like a Mother Language Day session run jointly with a club in Mumbai.

🤝

Fellowship & Fundraising

Installation ceremonies, goodwill visits between clubs, and public fundraisers like carnivals and comedy nights for local causes.

🏛️

Club Administration

DRR visits, club assemblies, and governance work that keeps all nine clubs compliant with District 3292's standards.

📣

Public Image

Documenting and sharing every project so the work reaches beyond the people in the room — social media, photography, storytelling.

About Us

Why Zone 7 exists.

District 3292 spans Nepal and Bhutan and is split into zones so that clubs get support at a scale that actually works — close enough for a Zonal Rotaract Representative to know every club president by name, small enough that a shared calendar and a WhatsApp group can hold it together, but connected enough to plug into 180+ clubs and 5,500+ Rotaractors districtwide.

Zone 7 is that layer for the Kathmandu Valley. It exists to do three things well: keep nine independently-run clubs coordinated instead of siloed, carry district standards and opportunities down to club level, and carry club achievements back up so they get recognized.

01
Coordinate, don't control

Each club sets its own goals and runs its own projects — Zone 7's job is to connect them, not direct them.

02
Raise the floor

Shared training (COTS), shared events, and a shared barometer mean even the newest club can hit district standards fast.

03
Make the work visible

From this site's project mosaic to the DRR visit reports, Zone 7 makes sure what clubs actually do doesn't stay invisible.
